# Destruction, repair, grievance, ecology and self-programming

This layer makes CYMONIA less like a one-way construction demo and more like a causal world.

## Nothing built is automatically permanent

Citizens may damage or dismantle:

- constructed buildings;
- transformed artifacts and ordinary objects;
- timber patches (trees);
- food patches (plants);
- stone, clay and ore patches.

Water is not treated as a destructible object.

Damage has physical resistance derived from material properties, the Citizen's physical capacity and the best tool-like artifact they carry.

When matter is destroyed it is not deleted. It becomes debris or salvage with provenance back to the damaged entity. Careful dismantling leaves more recoverable material than violent destruction.

## Destruction changes society

CYMONIA does not assume private property.

Social consequences instead come from evidence:

- actual repeated use of a structure;
- contribution to its construction;
- holding an object;
- explicit claims;
- direct local dependence on a resource patch.

Destroying something that matters to another Citizen can lower trust and affection, increase fear/stress and create a persistent grievance. Grievances decay slowly rather than disappearing after one tick.

A grievance can later make retaliation, confrontation or destruction more plausible. Repairing a damaged target can partially reduce a grievance.

## Overuse and destruction change ecology

Natural destruction leaves bounded environmental scars. Scars recover slowly and reduce nearby renewable-resource recovery while they remain severe.

Dense construction already reduces renewal through development pressure. This layer adds damage pressure, so land can become materially worse through repeated abuse.

## Repair is material, not cosmetic

Repair consumes a real held material object. Its mass is incorporated into the repaired target, condition improves, and total tracked matter remains conserved.

## Salvage is usable

Debris is a physical object. Citizens can perceive it, pick it up, drop it, transfer it, transform it and reuse it.

This means destruction can create an economy of salvage without inventing value or ownership.

## Citizens can program themselves

Citizens now have bounded persistent in-world programs.

A program is a small declarative behavior graph:

- a trigger;
- a cooldown;
- up to six canonical action steps;
- selectors that resolve only entities the Citizen can know.

Programs can be learned from repeated successful routines or proposed by Workers AI as part of strategic cognition.

The runtime deliberately does **not** execute arbitrary JavaScript, shell commands, network calls or host code from a Citizen. Self-programming changes the Citizen's behavior inside CYMONIA while the sovereign kernel still enforces physics, knowledge boundaries, mass conservation and persistence.

That boundary is what makes open-ended behavior compatible with a durable sandbox.

## The rule

A Citizen may decide to destroy, repair, retaliate, salvage or automate a routine.

The decision is theirs.

The consequences are the world's.
